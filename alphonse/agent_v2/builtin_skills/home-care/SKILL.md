# Home care

Help prioritize upkeep and prepare safe, verifiable next actions. A finite repair
or renovation effort can also use project-management; routine upkeep needs no project.

## Workflow

1. Identify the area/device, symptoms or desired routine, urgency and constraints.
   Separate observed symptoms from suspected causes. For appliance-specific advice,
   obtain the model/manual and relevant service history through authorized sources.
   Do not infer maintenance intervals or chemical compatibility from a brand alone.
2. For routine care, propose tasks by frequency or trigger with supplies, effort,
   proposed owner and an observable completion check. Adapt to occupancy and workload
   rather than presenting every possible chore as required.
3. For faults, start with safe observations and manufacturer-approved user checks.
   Stop ordinary troubleshooting for signs of fire, gas, dangerous electrical faults
   or structural danger; direct people away from the hazard and toward appropriate
   local emergency/professional help. Do not propose bypassing safeguards, opening
   energized equipment or mixing cleaning chemicals.
4. For a service visit, prepare the symptom summary, model details, access window,
   questions, warranty information and quote comparison criteria. Use household-
   administration for receipt/warranty tracking and family-coordination for access.

## Missing information and operational readiness

Checklists and service-preparation drafts need no account or dependency. Ask for
the model or missing observation when it changes safety or advice; provide a general
plan with uncertainty labeled when specifics are unnecessary. Live device readings,
device commands, bookings and messages require an available authorized integration
or installed dependency and the intended account. Verify readiness before action;
do not equate a proposed routine with an installed automation or completed repair.
Never mark maintenance performed just because its reminder fired.

## Examples and verification

- “Make a manageable weekend cleaning routine.” Ask about priority areas and time,
  then propose a short checklist with observable results and optional deeper tasks.
- “The AC isn't cooling.” Ask for model, symptoms and hazard signs; consult the
  manual for safe checks, record observations and prepare a technician brief if needed.
- “Schedule the technician.” Resolve provider, service scope, cost authorization
  and access window before using an available booking capability.

Verify recommendations against the relevant manual when equipment-specific.
For performed work, use actual observations or a service report; for bookings or
device changes, read back the confirmed appointment or resulting device state.
Keep manuals with private annotations, serial numbers and live service history in
authorized household storage, outside the shipped package.
