# Family coordination

Turn shared commitments into an achievable plan with explicit owners and handoffs.
Everyday coordination needs no project. Use only context the requester may access;
sharing a plan does not authorize disclosing another member's private schedule.

## Workflow

1. Establish the planning window, local timezone, participants and fixed commitments.
   Distinguish confirmed availability from assumptions. Ask for dates/timezone when
   ambiguity would change a booking or reminder; a draft can label those gaps.
2. Find overlaps, travel/setup time, unassigned responsibilities and dependencies.
   Do not assume a family member has agreed to a chore or ride. Propose an owner
   and a backup or handoff where useful; identify who still needs to confirm.
3. Offer a concise schedule or checklist with time, responsibility and unresolved
   decisions. Prefer a workable adjustment to silently dropping a commitment.
4. For authorized updates, inspect the current destination first, apply only the
   requested change and read it back. Reconcile uncertain results before retrying
   so an interrupted request does not create duplicate events or reminders.

## Missing information and operational readiness

Planning and drafting work without accounts or extra software. Reading calendars,
creating events/reminders and sending messages require an available authorized
native capability and the intended member's account/data selection. Check readiness
and actual availability before offering an effect. If unavailable, supply the draft
and identify the setup needed; do not claim invites, messages or reminders exist.
Ask only for gaps that affect the next action, such as recipient, recurrence or
which shared calendar to use. Never substitute another member's account.

## Examples and verification

- “Organize next week's school pickups.” Gather school dismissal times, confirmed
  drivers and travel constraints; return assigned/proposed pickups and gaps.
- “We have practice and a repair visit at the same time.” Compare the windows,
  suggest a handoff, and leave the choice with the people involved.
- “Plan dinner around late practices.” Combine meals-groceries for food choices
  and shopping quantities; use this skill for timing and ownership.

Check that every commitment has a date/window and that proposed owners are labeled.
Report conflicts that remain. For external changes, verify destination, timezone,
participants and saved status from readback. A drafted schedule is planning evidence,
not evidence that anyone was notified or accepted an assignment.
