# Household administration

Turn paperwork into a clear inventory, deadlines and next actions while minimizing
exposure of private records. This skill supports organization, not professional
legal, tax or financial judgments.

## Workflow

1. Establish the requested scope and records the member may access. Ask for the
   relevant document/account when needed; do not search other members' private
   records or request credentials in conversation. Retrieve only necessary fields.
2. Extract issuer, document type, service period, amount/currency, due date, renewal
   terms and source location as relevant. Separate document facts from inference;
   mark illegible, missing or conflicting values for reconciliation.
3. Build a concise checklist with deadline, proposed owner, next action and source.
   Distinguish statement date, due date and auto-renewal date. Resolve timezone,
   recurrence and ambiguous dates before scheduling reminders or submissions.
4. For duplicate bills or receipts, compare identifiers and coverage periods before
   treating them as separate obligations. Prepare questions for the provider when
   evidence conflicts. Use qualified/current authoritative advice where legal,
   tax or financial interpretation is required; do not invent filing obligations.

## Missing information and operational readiness

Organizing supplied documents and drafting checklists need no connected account.
Fetching live statements, saving records, reminders, payments, cancellations and
submissions need an available authorized capability and the intended account/data
binding. Check destination, amount/currency, deadline and authorized effect scope
before action. A request to track a bill does not authorize paying it. If a service
or dependency is unavailable, give the prepared checklist and specific setup gap;
do not claim a bill is paid, a policy renewed or a form submitted.

Keep credentials in the integration store and live records/configuration in
authorized household data storage outside this package. Summaries should omit
unneeded account numbers and other members' private details. Sharing a reusable
workflow never shares the underlying documents or account access.

## Examples and verification

- “What needs renewing this month?” Use authorized records, identify coverage gaps
  and unverified dates, and separate automatic renewals from actions still required.
- “Track the warranty for this repair.” Capture terms, proof of purchase and source;
  combine home-care for maintenance actions that may affect coverage.
- “Remind me before this bill is due.” Resolve date/time and recipient, inspect
  existing reminders, then verify an authorized reminder was actually saved.

Check totals by currency and link each deadline to its source. A checklist proves
organization only. Verify saved records and external changes by readback, and
payments/submissions by confirmation or receipt. After an uncertain result, inspect
the destination before retrying to avoid duplicate payments or submissions.
