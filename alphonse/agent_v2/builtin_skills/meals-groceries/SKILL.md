# Meals and groceries

Make a practical menu and shopping plan that fits the people, time and ingredients.

## Workflow

1. Use known serving counts, dietary restrictions/allergies, available cooking time,
   budget and pantry information. Ask about unknown restrictions before finalizing
   meals for others. For an early draft, mark serving counts and pantry assumptions.
   Do not invent a pantry inventory or treat an old list as current stock.
2. Choose meals around the actual schedule and equipment. Reuse perishable ingredients
   across meals, distinguish planned leftovers from fresh portions and avoid double
   counting them. Combine family-coordination when timing or handoffs matter.
3. Consolidate ingredients by grocery category with quantities and units. Deduct only
   confirmed usable pantry stock. Keep incompatible units separate unless a reliable
   conversion is available; explain uncertain package sizes or substitutions.
4. Separate essentials from optional items. Check substitutions against restrictions;
   never promise an allergen-free product without its current ingredient information.
   For medical diets or uncertain food safety, identify the missing authoritative
   guidance rather than inventing a safe treatment or storage rule.

## Missing information and operational readiness

Menu drafting and grocery lists need no account or installed dependency. Current
prices, store stock, ordering and delivery need available authorized services and
the intended account. Mark estimates as estimates. Before a purchase, resolve store,
budget, address, substitutions and the authorized checkout scope. A request for a
list alone does not authorize buying it. If a service is missing, return a usable
list and explain the unavailable operation without claiming a cart/order was made.
Keep actual preferences, pantry records and order history in authorized household
records outside this package; do not write them into reusable instructions.

## Examples and verification

- “Three quick dinners for four; we have rice.” Confirm restrictions and usable
  rice quantity, then propose meals, prep time and a list with unknown stock marked.
- “Combine these two recipes into one shopping list.” Scale each recipe to its
  servings, sum compatible quantities and retain recipe-specific alternatives.
- “Order this list.” Inspect current cart and account readiness; reconcile existing
  items before adding any, then verify any authorized order through its receipt.

Cross-check the menu against restrictions, servings, time and ingredient coverage.
List assumptions and remaining choices. For saved lists, carts or purchases, read
back the destination and quantities; distinguish a saved cart from a paid order.
